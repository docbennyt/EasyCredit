# Customer Credit Score

## What is the Customer Credit Score?

EasyCredit's customer credit score is an **internal business guidance tool** that helps you decide:

- Who usually pays reliably
- Who needs follow-up
- Who is becoming risky
- Who should not receive more credit until payment
- Who is settled and trustworthy

## Important: This is NOT a Credit Bureau Score

⚠️ **What this score is NOT:**
- Not a formal credit bureau score
- Not a bank credit rating
- Not shared with other businesses
- Not a legal judgment

✅ **What this score IS:**
- Internal guidance for YOUR business
- Based on YOUR records with each customer
- Helps YOU make better decisions
- Updated as you add more records

## Understanding Risk Levels

Each customer is assigned a risk level based on their payment behavior:

### 🟢 Reliable (Score 85-100)
**Meaning:** Consistently pays on time

**Typical behavior:**
- Pays balances promptly
- Rarely overdue
- Good payment history
- Settled accounts regularly

**Your action:** Safe for normal credit

**Example:**
> **Mai T**  
> Balance: $0  
> Recent payment: Yes  
> Status: **Reliable**  
> "Usually pays on time"

---

### 🔵 Good (Score 70-84)
**Meaning:** Generally pays well, minor delays possible

**Typical behavior:**
- Usually pays within reasonable time
- Occasionally a few days late
- Overall positive history
- Active customer

**Your action:** Safe for credit with normal limits

**Example:**
> **Uncle Joe**  
> Balance: $8  
> Last payment: 5 days ago  
> Status: **Good**  
> "Good payment history"

---

### 🟡 Watch (Score 50-69)
**Meaning:** Needs attention, some risk

**Typical behavior:**
- Sometimes late on payments
- Growing balance
- No recent payments
- Inconsistent settlement

**Your action:** Monitor closely, consider limits

**Example:**
> **Rudo**  
> Balance: $25  
> Last payment: 35 days ago  
> Status: **Watch**  
> "No recent payment (30+ days)"

---

### 🔴 High Risk (Score 0-49)
**Meaning:** Significant risk, avoid more credit

**Typical behavior:**
- Overdue by many days
- No recent payments
- Multiple unpaid records
- Growing debt

**Your action:** Follow up immediately, no new credit

**Example:**
> **Mr Moyo**  
> Balance: $15  
> Overdue: 6 days  
> Last payment: Never  
> Status: **High Risk**  
> "Overdue and becoming risky"

---

### ⚪ Settled / Change Owed
**Meaning:** No credit balance, or you owe them

**Typical behavior:**
- Zero balance (settled)
- OR negative balance (you owe change)

**Your action:** Return change if applicable

**Example:**
> **Tawanda**  
> Balance: -$2 (you owe change)  
> Status: **You owe change**  
> "Business owes customer change"

---

## How the Score is Calculated

The score starts at **100** (perfect) and adjusts based on these factors:

### Factors That Lower the Score

**1. Current Outstanding Balance**
- High balance (>$100): -20 points
- Medium balance ($50-100): -10 points
- *Why:* More money at risk

**2. Overdue Status**
- Overdue: -5 points per day (max -50)
- *Why:* Late payment increases risk

**3. Number of Unpaid Records**
- Multiple unpaid items: additional penalty
- *Why:* Pattern of non-payment

**4. No Payment History**
- Never paid: -15 points
- *Why:* Unknown reliability

**5. No Recent Payment Activity**
- No payment in 30+ days: -10 points
- *Why:* Inactive or avoiding payment

### Factors That Raise the Score

**1. Recent Settlement**
- Paid within last 7 days: +10 points (max 100)
- *Why:* Shows active payment

**2. Consistent Payment History**
- Regular payments: +5 points (max 100)
- *Why:* Reliable customer

**3. Change Trust (Negative Balance)**
- You owe them change: +10 points (max 100)
- *Why:* Clean relationship

**4. Full Settlement**
- Zero balance after payment: +5 points (max 100)
- *Why:* Responsible behavior

### Score Range

Final score is clamped between **0 and 100**.

## How to Use the Score

### ✅ DO:

**Use as guidance**
- Score helps prioritize who to follow up with
- Score helps decide credit limits
- Score shows trends over time

**Combine with personal knowledge**
- You know your customers better than a number
- Consider special circumstances
- Use your judgment

**Update through actions**
- Scores improve with payments
- Scores worsen with delays
- More records = more accurate score

### ❌ DON'T:

**Rely solely on the score**
- Score is guidance, not gospel
- Consider relationship and context
- Use business judgment

**Share scores externally**
- Scores are private to your business
- Not for public sharing
- Not a legal document

**Judge new customers harshly**
- New customers have limited history
- Give them a fair chance
- Build history over time

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can customers see their score?**  
A: No, scores are internal to your business only.

**Q: How often does the score update?**  
A: Scores recalculate whenever you view the customer or add a record.

**Q: Can I override the score?**  
A: The score is automatic, but you always use your own judgment for decisions.

**Q: What if the score seems wrong?**  
A: Check the customer's payment history. The score reflects recorded behavior.

**Q: Do new customers start at 100?**  
A: New customers start at 100 but may quickly adjust based on their first transactions.

**Q: Can a score go back to 100 after being low?**  
A: Yes! Consistent payments and settlements improve scores over time.

## Tips for Managing Customer Risk

**For "Watch" Customers:**
- Follow up before they become overdue
- Set shorter due dates
- Request partial payments
- Keep credit limits lower

**For "High Risk" Customers:**
- Immediate follow-up required
- No new credit until settled
- Consider cash-only terms
- Document all communications

**For "Reliable" Customers:**
- Reward with flexible terms
- Consider higher limits if appropriate
- Maintain good relationship
- Acknowledge their reliability

- Make informed decisions

Your knowledge of your customers, combined with the score, creates the best approach.
